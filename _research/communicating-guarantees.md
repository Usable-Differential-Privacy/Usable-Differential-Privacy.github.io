Real-world deployments often disclose too little information for people to understand the protection being offered. Reporting a single value such as epsilon can omit important context, invite misleading comparisons, and undermine trust.

Our research investigates what information a responsible differential privacy disclosure should contain, how it should be presented to audiences with different technical backgrounds, and how the accuracy of those disclosures can be verified. This work brings together the perspectives of differential privacy experts, system developers, data practitioners, policymakers, and members of the public.

A central goal is to develop a standardized differential privacy certificate or privacy label that:

- communicates the parameters and assumptions that define a deployment's privacy guarantee;
- presents information in layers appropriate for technical and nontechnical audiences;
- connects public-facing claims to verifiable properties of the underlying system; and
- can be generated and shared using practical software tools.
